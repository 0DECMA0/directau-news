Fortescue Metals Group executive chairman Andrew Forrest has issued a stern ultimatum to his global workforce, declaring that those who participate in or tolerate sexual harassment have no place within the mining giant. The internal directive follows the commencement of a high-profile class action lawsuit alleging systemic workplace misconduct, marking a pivotal moment for the organisation's corporate culture.

In an all-staff email, the billionaire mining magnate reinforced a zero-tolerance policy, stating that any employee who believes such conduct is permissible is in the "wrong company." This move comes as the Australian resources sector faces increasing scrutiny over its historic failure to protect female workers from predatory behaviour in remote mining camps.

> "The true measure of a corporate titan is no longer found in its balance sheet, but in the psychological safety it provides to its most vulnerable workers."

The legal proceedings signal a growing trend of industrial accountability within the Pilbara and beyond, as victims seek systemic redress for historical grievances. For Fortescue, the challenge lies in translating Mr Forrest’s uncompromising rhetoric into tangible cultural change that resonates across every level of the business operation.
