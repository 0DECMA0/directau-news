Colleen Harkin, a prominent Liberal Party member and the education director at a high-profile think tank, has officially resigned from the party. The departure comes just one week after her presence at a Pauline Hanson’s One Nation fundraiser in Melbourne sparked a significant internal backlash and raised questions regarding party discipline.

Ms Harkin’s exit highlights the ongoing ideological friction within the Victorian division of the Liberal Party as it struggles to define its borders against more right-leaning minor parties. While Harkin had previously sought pre-selection for the party, her decision to publicly support a rival organisation's event made her continued membership untenable for the leadership team seeking to project a unified front.

> "The integrity of a major political brand rests on the unwavering commitment of its members; when the line between curiosity and endorsement blurs, the party's core identity faces an existential threat."

This resignation marks a notable loss of a policy specialist for the Liberals, yet it serves as a firm reassertion of party boundaries. As the political landscape in Victoria becomes increasingly fractured, the party must now navigate the fallout of losing a veteran operative while attempting to shore up its base against further defections to the crossbench.
