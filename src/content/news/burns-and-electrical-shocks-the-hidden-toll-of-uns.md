More than 80 Australians have suffered significant physical harm from faulty products purchased via online marketplaces this year, according to the latest figures from the nation’s consumer watchdog. The Australian Competition and Consumer Commission (ACCC) has flagged a worrying rise in incidents involving severe burns and electrical shocks, highlighting a critical gap in the safety oversight of global digital storefronts operating within our borders.

While the official tally of injuries for 2025 remains in the double digits, consumer advocacy groups warn that the recorded data represents only the tip of the iceberg. Many incidents go unreported as shoppers struggle to navigate complex refund processes or find themselves unable to hold offshore sellers accountable under domestic Australian Consumer Law, leaving many to deal with the aftermath of faulty goods in private.

> "The digital frontier remains a regulatory blind spot where the convenience of a bargain often disguises a systemic failure to protect the physical wellbeing of the Australian public."

As pressure mounts on the federal government to implement stricter mandatory safety standards for third-party platforms, the ACCC is urging citizens to exercise extreme caution. Without legislative reform that places the onus of safety directly on the hosting marketplace, the toll of preventable physical incidents is expected to climb as e-commerce continues to dominate the retail landscape.
