Regional Australians are grappling with a staggering financial burden as new data reveals air travel costs per kilometre are 173 per cent higher than on major metropolitan routes. A scathing interim report released by the Productivity Commission has highlighted a profound disconnect in the nation's aviation sector, sparking urgent calls for a rethink of how regional connectivity is maintained and funded.

The investigation found that while competitive pressures on trunk routes between capital cities have kept prices relatively stable, those living in the bush are being penalised by a lack of market competition. Thin routes, often serviced by a single carrier, have seen fares skyrocket, making essential travel for health, business, and education increasingly inaccessible for rural communities.

> "The tyranny of distance in Australia has evolved into a fiscal barrier, where the cost of a domestic short-haul flight can now outweigh the economic benefit of the journey itself."

Industry stakeholders are now urging the Federal Government to consider immediate regulatory interventions to address what they describe as a clear market failure. As the Commission continues its inquiry, the focus shifts to whether increased subsidies or mandated competition requirements will be enough to bridge the growing divide between city and regional Australia.
