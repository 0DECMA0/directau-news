King Charles III has issued a stern warning to the world's premier technology architects regarding the "existential dangers" posed by artificial intelligence, urging global leaders to ensure the technology does not fall into the wrong hands. Addressing a high-level assembly of industry pioneers, the monarch emphasised that while the potential for progress is immense, the risks associated with unbridled digital autonomy require immediate and rigorous international oversight.

The royal intervention comes at a critical juncture as nations scramble to establish regulatory frameworks for a technology moving faster than legislation can follow. His Majesty highlighted that the protection of democratic values and the safety of citizens must remain paramount, particularly as malicious actors seek to exploit advancements in machine learning for destabilising purposes.

> "The boundary between human ingenuity and autonomous digital agency has reached a critical juncture, where the cost of a misstep could fundamentally alter the trajectory of our civilisation."

As the summit concludes, the King's remarks serve as a significant moral benchmark for the tech sector, shifting the conversation from mere commercial viability to ethical responsibility. Industry leaders now face the daunting task of harmonising the pursuit of innovation with the collective security of a world increasingly reliant on complex algorithms.
