A participant has been hospitalised with a serious facial injury after a harrowing encounter during the San Fermin bull run festival in Pamplona, northern Spain. Local authorities confirmed the runner was struck by a bull's horn during the high-speed transit through the city's narrow cobblestone streets. Emergency medical teams stationed along the route provided immediate assistance before transferring the individual to a regional trauma centre for urgent intervention.

This latest incident highlights the enduring risks associated with the centuries-old 'encierro' tradition, which attracts thousands of international thrill-seekers to the Navarre region annually. Despite modern safety enhancements and a significant security presence, the inherent unpredictability of the half-tonne fighting bulls remains a primary concern for event organisers. The festival, while a cornerstone of Spanish cultural identity, continues to face global scrutiny regarding the physical toll on its participants.

> "The ancient spectacle of the bull run represents a complex intersection of cultural heritage and extreme physical risk, where the margin for error remains perilously thin."

Medical officials have not yet released the identity or nationality of the injured party, pending notification of kin. As the week-long festivities proceed, local authorities have reiterated the necessity for participants to adhere strictly to safety protocols. The incident has once again sparked debate over the balance between preserving historical traditions and ensuring the contemporary safety of the public.
