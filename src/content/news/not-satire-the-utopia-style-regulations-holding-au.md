The Productivity Commission has issued a stern directive to state and territory governments, asserting that Australia’s escalating housing affordability crisis can only be resolved by adopting a fundamental "build mindset." The latest report highlights how a labyrinth of bureaucratic hurdles and planning restrictions—likened to the satirical world of the television series *Utopia*—is actively preventing the construction of essential new dwellings across the nation.

According to the commission, the current regulatory framework has prioritised administrative process over the practical necessity of housing supply, leading to a significant shortfall in available stock. This bottleneck has not only driven prices to record highs but has also stifled the ability of the private sector to respond to the growing needs of a rising population, leaving many Australians locked out of the property market.

> "The chasm between legislative intent and the physical reality of construction has become so vast that the Australian dream is being quietly dismantled by the weight of its own red tape."

To rectify this systemic failure, the report calls for a streamlined approach to land use and zoning that incentivises development rather than obstructing it. Experts argue that without a concerted effort to dismantle these 'Utopia-style' regulations, the government’s ambitious housing targets will remain out of reach, further compromising the economic stability of future generations.
