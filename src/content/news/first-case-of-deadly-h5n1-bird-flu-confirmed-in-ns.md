Biosecurity authorities have confirmed the first recorded case of the high-pathogenicity H5N1 avian influenza strain in New South Wales, identified in a giant petrel found on the state’s Mid North Coast. The discovery marks a critical shift in the national ecological landscape, as Australia was previously one of the few regions globally to have avoided this specific strain of the virus in its wild bird populations.

The Department of Primary Industries (DPI) has initiated emergency surveillance protocols following the laboratory confirmation, with field teams monitoring the coastline for further incidents. While the virus has caused significant disruption to avian life internationally, local officials are focusing on containment and the protection of both native biodiversity and the domestic poultry industry.

> "The arrival of H5N1 on our shores is more than a biosecurity milestone; it is a profound test of our environmental resilience and a clear signal that geographical isolation is no longer a guaranteed shield against global ecological threats."

Residents and commercial landholders are being urged to maintain strict hygiene standards and report any sightings of multiple sick or deceased birds to the Emergency Animal Disease Hotline. Public health experts have emphasised that while the risk to human health remains low, the potential for widespread impact on unique Australian fauna necessitates a coordinated and rapid inter-agency response.
