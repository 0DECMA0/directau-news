The Queensland Government has granted final approval for a landmark 2,200-home redevelopment on Brisbane’s Northshore, effectively green-lighting the transformation of the city’s last major industrial waterfront site. While the multi-billion dollar project is set to revitalise a prime precinct near the CBD, it has immediately drawn fire from housing advocates for failing to include a single affordable or social housing dwelling.

Member for Clayfield Tim Nicholls has backed the project, describing the former industrial tract as a premier opportunity situated close to essential infrastructure and lifestyle hubs. However, the decision to bypass mandatory affordable housing quotas comes at a critical juncture for the Sunshine State, as the capital continues to grapple with a vacancy crisis and record-breaking rental increases. Critics argue that the exclusion of low-cost options in such a central location represents a significant failure in long-term urban social planning.

> "The conversion of our last remaining inner-city industrial frontier into an exclusive high-yield enclave represents a missed opportunity to bridge the widening chasm between urban supply and genuine social accessibility."

As the machinery prepares to move into the Northshore site, the focus shifts to the broader implications for Brisbane’s middle-income and essential workers. With the 2032 Olympic Games on the horizon, the development stands as a stark monument to the ongoing tension between rapid urban expansion and the pressing need for inclusive housing solutions in the heart of the city.
