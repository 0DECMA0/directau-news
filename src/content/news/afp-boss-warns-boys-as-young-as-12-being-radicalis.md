The Australian Federal Police (AFP) has issued a stark warning regarding a disturbing shift in national security, revealing that children as young as 12 are being radicalised within the confines of their own homes. Commissioner Reece Kershaw disclosed that fresh intelligence has fundamentally challenged long-held assumptions about extremist recruitment, highlighting a shift toward younger, tech-savvy demographics who are increasingly targeted through digital platforms rather than traditional physical networks.

This latest data suggests that the conventional model of radicalisation—once thought to occur in secluded meeting spaces or among older cohorts—has been superseded by a pervasive, invisible threat. The process often occurs behind closed doors, away from the direct observation of authorities and even immediate family members, necessitating a comprehensive recalibration of domestic counter-terrorism strategies and community engagement programs.

> "The digital landscape has transformed the domestic hearth into a frontline, where isolation serves as the primary recruitment tool for extremist ideologies."

As the AFP continues to monitor these emerging patterns, the emphasis has shifted toward the necessity of parental vigilance and early community-led intervention. Law enforcement experts argue that addressing the root causes of this ideological shift requires more than just surveillance; it demands a robust social framework capable of identifying vulnerable youth before they are drawn into a path of potential harm.
