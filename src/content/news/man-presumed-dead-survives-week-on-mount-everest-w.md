A Sherpa mountain guide previously feared lost to the elements on Mount Everest has remarkably reappeared at base camp, having survived seven days in the high-altitude 'death zone' without supplementary oxygen or sustenance. The individual, who went missing during a treacherous descent last week, was spotted by fellow climbers as he made a final, desperate crawl towards the safety of the lower camps earlier this morning.

International search teams had initially suspended operations after forty-eight hours, citing the extreme physiological toll of the altitude and the plummeting temperatures as factors that rendered survival beyond that window statistically impossible. The guide’s return defies current medical understanding of human endurance in environments where oxygen levels are insufficient to sustain basic organ function for prolonged periods.

> "In the face of the mountain’s absolute indifference, the resilience of the human spirit remains the only unpredictable variable in modern exploration."

The guide is currently undergoing intensive treatment for severe frostbite and acute exhaustion at a specialist facility. While his recovery is expected to be lengthy, his survival has sparked a rigorous debate among the Himalayan climbing community regarding the standard duration of search-and-rescue missions and the limits of human physiological capacity at the roof of the world.
