The Hobart City Council has taken a decisive step toward mitigating the city’s housing crisis by voting to advance strict new regulations on whole-home short-stay accommodation. The proposed amendments to the local planning scheme aim to restrict the conversion of existing residential dwellings into holiday rentals, effectively prioritising the availability of long-term housing for local residents and essential workers.

This policy shift follows years of mounting concern regarding the impact of digital platforms on the Tasmanian capital’s rental market, which has consistently ranked as one of the most unaffordable in Australia. Under the new framework, the council seeks to curb the commercialisation of residential suburbs while maintaining a provision that allows homeowners to rent out individual rooms within their primary residence, a compromise intended to balance economic activity with community stability.

> "The tension between a flourishing visitor economy and the fundamental right to shelter has reached a tipping point, forcing local government to intervene in the free market to preserve the social fabric of our city."

The initiative now moves to the Tasmanian Planning Commission for final assessment and a formal public exhibition phase. While the council’s vote represents a significant victory for housing advocates, the ultimate implementation of the ban remains subject to state-level approval, a process that will be scrutinised by property investors and the tourism sector alike as Hobart grapples with its future as a liveable city.
