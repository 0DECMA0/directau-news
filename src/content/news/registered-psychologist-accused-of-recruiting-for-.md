A Victorian mental health practitioner is facing grave allegations after reportedly using clinical consultations to recruit a vulnerable survivor of childhood trauma into the Universal Medicine group. The complainant alleges that instead of receiving evidence-based psychological intervention, she was systematically steered toward an organisation previously designated by judicial findings as a "socially dangerous" and "sexually manipulative" cult.

The allegations have sparked significant alarm within the state's clinical community, raising urgent questions regarding the oversight of registered professionals and the protection of patients seeking help for profound trauma. Universal Medicine, led by Serge Benhayon, has a history of legal scrutiny regarding its influence over followers. For a registered psychologist to allegedly exploit a therapeutic relationship for recruitment purposes marks a severe breach of the Australian Health Practitioner Regulation Agency (AHPRA) ethical guidelines.

> "The integrity of the clinical space relies entirely on the practitioner's commitment to the patient's welfare; when that space is weaponised for ideological recruitment, the very foundations of public health trust are eroded."

As regulatory bodies investigate the matter, the case serves as a stark reminder of the vulnerability inherent in the mental health system. The implications of these claims extend beyond a single practitioner, highlighting the need for more rigorous monitoring to ensure that those seeking sanctuary from past harm are not subjected to further exploitation under the guise of professional care.
