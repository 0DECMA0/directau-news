The Bonorong Wildlife Sanctuary has officially terminated its partnership with the national rescue service WIRES, labelling the organisation’s Tasmanian hotline operations an "absolute disaster." The decision marks a significant fracture in the state’s conservation network, as local experts claim the centralised system is failing to provide the rapid response required for distressed fauna.

According to senior management at the Brighton-based sanctuary, the decision to stop redirecting callers to the WIRES hotline stems from consistent reports of unacceptable delays. Stakeholders argue that baby animals and injured wildlife are being left in precarious situations for far too long, as the mainland-managed administrative process struggles to coordinate effectively with Tasmanian-based volunteer carers.

> "The friction between mainland administrative logistics and the urgent, heartbeat-driven reality of regional wildlife rescue exposes a systemic failure that Tasmania’s fauna can no longer afford to endure."

In response to the growing backlog, Bonorong will now prioritise direct local communication to ensure that rescued animals reach professional care within the critical window of survival. While WIRES has previously defended its model as a means of streamlining national rescue efforts, the move by one of Tasmania's most prominent sanctuaries highlights a deepening divide over how best to protect the state’s unique and vulnerable wildlife populations.
