Hundreds of protesters gathered on the steps of Parliament House in Adelaide today, demanding the immediate withdrawal of a controversial data centre proposal slated for the Adelaide Hills region. The rally, which saw a significant turnout from local residents and environmental advocates, marks an escalation in the community’s campaign against the large-scale industrial development.

The demonstrators argue that the proposed facility is fundamentally at odds with the heritage-listed landscape and rural character of the Adelaide Hills. Concerns were raised regarding the project's environmental footprint, specifically its energy consumption and the potential impact on local water resources, as the community calls on the state government to prioritise conservation over industrial expansion.

> "The tension between technological infrastructure and regional preservation has reached a critical flashpoint, highlighting a growing disconnect between industrial ambition and the sanctity of community heritage."

While the company behind the proposal maintains that the data centre will provide essential infrastructure and create local jobs, opposition remains steadfast. Planning authorities are now facing increased pressure to review the project’s viability as the South Australian government considers the broader implications of allowing such developments within protected agricultural and residential zones.
