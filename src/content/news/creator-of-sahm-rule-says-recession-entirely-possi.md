The economist behind the globally recognised recession indicator, Claudia Sahm, has warned that a significant economic downturn is now an entirely possible outcome for Australia. Speaking on the current trajectory of the domestic labour market, the former Federal Reserve economist noted that while the nation has shown resilience, the steady uptick in unemployment figures suggests the economy is entering a delicate and potentially hazardous phase.

Historically, the Sahm Rule identifies the early stages of a recession when the three-month moving average of the unemployment rate rises by 0.5 percentage points or more relative to its low during the previous twelve months. As the Reserve Bank of Australia maintains its restrictive monetary stance to combat persistent inflation, analysts are increasingly concerned that the lag effect of high interest rates may be cooling the jobs market faster than anticipated, pushing the local economy toward a tipping point.

> "The fragility of the labour market often serves as the final domino to fall, transitioning a controlled slowdown into a systemic contraction that defies traditional policy interventions."

While Treasury officials remain cautiously optimistic about achieving a soft landing, the warning from such a high-profile figure underscores the narrowing path for the RBA. The focus now shifts to upcoming quarterly data, which will determine if the current softening of employment is a temporary correction or the beginning of a broader economic retreat that could impact households across the country.
