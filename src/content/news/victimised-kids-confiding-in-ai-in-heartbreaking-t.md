The Australian Federal Police has issued a stark warning regarding a "heartbreaking" trend where children, targeted by online predators, are seeking solace in artificial intelligence rather than human authorities. Commissioner Reece Kershaw stated that an increasing number of young victims are confiding in chatbots following incidents of digital exploitation, highlighting a critical breakdown in traditional support structures.

As digital platforms become the primary playground for the youth, the reliance on automated responses poses a significant risk to both the physical safety and psychological recovery of victims. Law enforcement officials express concern that these AI tools lack the mandatory reporting capabilities and emotional nuance required to handle such sensitive matters, potentially allowing offenders to remain undetected.

> "The rise of the digital confessional marks a profound shift in the landscape of childhood trauma, where algorithms are being asked to carry the weight of human justice and healing."

The Commissioner is now calling for an urgent intervention from parents and the broader community to bridge this communication gap. By fostering a safer environment for disclosure, authorities hope to ensure that the next generation turns to their protectors rather than their processors in times of crisis.
