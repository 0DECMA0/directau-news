Premier Coal has commenced the process of issuing redundancy forms to its workforce in Collie, signalling a drastic reduction in operations as the company moves to abolish night shifts. This strategic retreat will see one of Western Australia’s final coal bastions slash its production capacity by nearly half, a move that industrial representatives claim will lead to significant job losses across the region.

The operational downscaling comes as the state continues its pivot away from coal-fired power, aiming for a full transition by the end of the decade. While management frames the decision as a necessary realignment with current demand, the move has sent shockwaves through the South West community, which has long relied on the mine as a primary economic engine for generations of local families.

> "The erosion of shift work in the Collie basin marks more than just a logistical shift; it signals the structural dismantling of a regional economic pillar."

As the curtains begin to close on the era of heavy fossil fuel extraction in Western Australia, the focus remains squarely on the adequacy of support for displaced workers. For a town that has powered the state for over a century, these redundancies represent a sobering milestone in an inevitable, yet painful, industrial transition.
