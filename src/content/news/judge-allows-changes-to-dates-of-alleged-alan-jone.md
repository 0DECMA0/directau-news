A New South Wales judge has granted a prosecution request to amend the alleged dates of several indecent assault offences in the criminal trial of veteran radio broadcaster Alan Jones. The ruling, delivered during the second week of proceedings in Sydney, follows testimony from witnesses that introduced significant inconsistencies into the previously established timeline of events.

The decision to alter the date ranges comes as the court navigates the complexities of historical allegations. Legal counsel for the Crown argued that the amendments were essential to ensure the charges align with the evidence provided by witnesses under oath, despite objections from the defence regarding the timing of such a fundamental shift in the case's framework.

> "In the pursuit of justice for historical matters, the court must often reconcile the fallibility of human memory with the uncompromising requirements of legal documentation."

The presiding judge noted that the trial would continue under the revised indictments, maintaining that the core nature of the allegations remains unchanged. As the proceedings move forward, the focus remains on the reliability of witness accounts and the court's ability to provide a fair hearing amidst these evolving procedural adjustments.
