Six people have been confirmed deceased and 129 remain unaccounted for after a passenger ferry encountered severe weather conditions in the Java Sea. The vessel, which was carrying 243 souls, departed from Surabaya, Indonesia's second-largest city, bound for Banjarmasin in Kalimantan before falling victim to turbulent maritime conditions.

Indonesian authorities have deployed naval assets and search teams to the region, though operations are currently being hindered by the same volatile weather that caused the initial disaster. Family members have gathered at Surabaya’s port as the window for finding survivors in the treacherous waters begins to narrow.

> "This disaster underscores the inherent risks of maritime navigation in the Indonesian archipelago, where the intersection of ageing infrastructure and unpredictable tropical weather continues to claim lives."

As the search continues, questions are being raised regarding safety protocols and the decision to sail despite the forecast. International maritime observers are monitoring the situation closely as the region grapples with one of its most significant sea tragedies in recent years.
