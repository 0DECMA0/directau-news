A federal senator has delivered a scathing assessment of the cruise industry, labelling vessels operated by Carnival "floating sweatshops" following allegations of systemic labour exploitation. The claims have prompted the New South Wales Anti-Slavery Commissioner to call for urgent legislative reform to protect vulnerable workers operating in international waters but docking at local ports.

The allegations surfaced during a formal inquiry where former staff detailed gruelling shifts, subpar living conditions, and remuneration packages that fall significantly below Australian workplace standards. While these cruise lines often operate under international maritime law and flags of convenience, the proximity of their operations to the Australian economy has ignited a fierce debate over the ethical responsibilities of multi-billion dollar corporations reaping record profits from the local tourism sector.

> "This intersection of international legal loopholes and corporate profit margins creates a volatile grey zone where human dignity is too often traded for the sake of a cheaper holiday ticket."

Commissioner James Cockayne has suggested that the current regulatory framework is fundamentally insufficient to address modern slavery risks at sea. As the Senate continues its investigation, pressure is mounting on the federal government to implement stricter oversight mechanisms, ensuring that any vessel benefiting from Australian infrastructure adheres to more rigorous humanitarian standards.
