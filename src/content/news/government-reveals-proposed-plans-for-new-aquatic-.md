The Queensland Government has officially unveiled the preliminary designs for the centrepiece aquatic venue of the 2032 Olympic and Paralympic Games, confirming that a consortium of three leading architectural firms has been appointed to lead the project. Situated at the historic Centenary Pool site in Spring Hill, the proposed development aims to reconcile world-class competitive standards with the long-term recreational needs of the Brisbane community.

The project marks a pivotal step in the state’s multi-billion-dollar infrastructure pipeline, with the selected firms tasked with creating a sustainable facility that honours the site’s heritage listing. Current renders indicate a sophisticated integration of modern glass and steel elements designed to maximise spectator capacity while maintaining the architectural integrity of the surrounding parklands.

> "The redesign of the Centenary Pool precinct is a high-stakes balancing act between preserving local heritage and meeting the relentless technical demands of a global sporting event."

As the state moves toward the final planning phase, officials have emphasised that the facility must serve as a functional 'legacy' asset long after the closing ceremony. Public consultation will be prioritised in the coming months to ensure the transition from a high-performance arena to a public leisure hub remains seamless and economically viable for future generations.
