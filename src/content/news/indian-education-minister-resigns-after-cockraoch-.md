Indian Union Minister for Education, Dharmendra Pradhan, has tendered his resignation following weeks of intensifying civil unrest spearheaded by the "Cockroach" movement. The departure comes in the wake of systemic failures regarding national examination integrity, a crisis that has left the academic futures of millions of students hanging in the balance across the subcontinent.

The protest movement gained significant momentum after a series of high-profile question paper leaks came to light, severely undermining the credibility of the National Testing Agency. While Mr Pradhan initially stated he would take full responsibility for the administrative lapses, public demand for a leadership change became insurmountable as demonstrators took to the streets across major metropolitan hubs to voice their frustration.

> "When the sanctity of merit is compromised by bureaucratic frailty, the resulting deficit of trust becomes a burden no single minister can afford to carry."

The Indian government must now move swiftly to restore faith in its academic vetting processes and appoint a successor capable of navigating this institutional crisis. Observers suggest that this resignation marks a pivotal moment for the current administration, highlighting the growing influence of youth-led activism in demanding accountability within the world’s largest democracy.
