Residents of Moss Vale have brought their grievances before the New South Wales Land and Environment Court, marking a significant milestone in a six-year dispute over a proposed plastics recycling facility. The community, situated in the Southern Highlands, argues that the prolonged legal battle has fundamentally compromised their wellbeing, with many citing severe impacts on their mental health and financial stability since the project's inception.

The conflict stems from the proposed development of a large-scale industrial site, which locals claim is incompatible with the regional character of the town. During testimonies, residents detailed how the persistent uncertainty regarding the plant’s approval has forced them to delay retirement and endure chronic stress. The court heard that the social fabric of the neighbourhood is under significant strain as the industrial proposal looms over long-term residential security.

> "When legal technicalities overshadow the lived experience of a community, the true cost of industrial progress is measured not in dollars, but in the erosion of public peace and personal health."

As the Land and Environment Court continues to weigh the technical merits of the recycling plant against the personal testimonies provided, the outcome remains a pivotal moment for NSW planning laws. The final decision is expected to set a precedent for how community impact is balanced against industrial infrastructure in regional hubs. The case remains under deliberation as the bench considers the extensive evidence submitted by both the developers and the local collective.
