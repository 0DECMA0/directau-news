An interim report released today has confirmed that a runaway coal train reached speeds of 90 kilometres per hour during an uncontrolled 20-kilometre journey. The Australian Transport Safety Bureau’s preliminary findings indicate the heavy consist travelled well in excess of established safety limits before eventually rolling backwards for a further five kilometres to a final halt.

Investigators are currently scrutinising the mechanical failures and operational protocols that allowed the freight vehicle to lose braking authority. While the incident resulted in no injuries or loss of life, the sheer scale of the momentum involved has raised urgent questions regarding the resilience of regional rail infrastructure and the effectiveness of emergency stopping procedures.

> "The sheer kinetic energy of a runaway coal consist represents a significant breach of our network’s safety integrity, exposing a critical vulnerability that demands immediate regulatory oversight."

The focus of the ongoing inquiry has now shifted to the technical logs and the sequence of events that led to the initial loss of control. A final report, expected in the coming months, will provide formal recommendations to prevent such a high-risk event from recurring on the state’s transport corridors.
