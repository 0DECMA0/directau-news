Woodside Energy’s anticipated withdrawal from the Greater Sunrise oil and gas fields has triggered warnings that China is poised to secure a dominant strategic foothold in the Timor Sea. Industry analysts suggest that Beijing's state-backed enterprises are the most likely candidates to fill the investment vacuum left by the Australian energy giant, a development that could fundamentally shift the geopolitical landscape of the region.

The Greater Sunrise project, situated between Timor-Leste and Australia, has remained undeveloped for decades due to a protracted dispute over whether the gas should be piped to Darwin or a new facility on Timor-Leste's southern coast. With Woodside signaling its departure to prioritise other global assets, the government in Dili is increasingly looking toward northern partners who are willing to finance the infrastructure required for local processing—a move Canberra has historically viewed with caution.

> "Nature abhors a vacuum, and in the high-stakes theatre of Indo-Pacific energy security, an Australian retreat often serves as an invitation for Beijing’s long-term strategic expansion."

This potential realignment poses a significant challenge for Canberra’s diplomatic standing in the neighbourhood. As Timor-Leste seeks economic sovereignty through resource development, the transition from a Perth-based operator to a Chinese state entity would carry profound implications for regional maritime security and Australia's long-term influence over its northern approaches.
