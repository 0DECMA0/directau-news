Acclaimed author Tim Winton has joined local advocates in condemning a proposal for a new deep-sea port in Exmouth, arguing that the industrial project poses an existential threat to the Gascoyne region’s ecological integrity. The development, which aims to transform the town into a primary trading hub, has ignited a fierce debate over the balance between economic stimulation and environmental preservation.

Proponents of the facility suggest the infrastructure would bolster the regional economy by providing essential services to the shipping and resources sectors. However, critics, led by Winton, maintain that the delicate marine environment—adjacent to the world-renowned Ningaloo Reef—cannot withstand the impact of large-scale industrial operations and increased vessel traffic.

> "The tension between industrial ambition and environmental stewardship in Exmouth highlights a fundamental choice about what we value most in the unique Australian landscape."

As the state government weighs the projected economic benefits against the potential for permanent ecological degradation, the community remains deeply divided. Winton's intervention adds significant cultural weight to the opposition, ensuring that the future of the Gascoyne remains a national point of contention for both policymakers and conservationists alike.
