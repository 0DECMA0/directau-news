Jon Henricks, the legendary sprinter who spearheaded Australia’s golden age of swimming at the 1956 Melbourne Olympics, has passed away at the age of 91. A pioneer of the sport, Henricks secured two gold medals in the 100-metre freestyle and the 4x200-metre relay, etching his name into the annals of international sporting history.

His victory in Melbourne was part of a historic clean sweep for the host nation, as he led teammates John Devitt and Gary Chapman to the podium. Beyond the medals, Henricks was a visionary athlete, famously becoming the first man to break the 55-second barrier in the 100-metre freestyle, a feat that fundamentally altered the technical expectations of the discipline.

> "Henricks did not merely race against his peers; he redefined the physical limits of the pool, leaving a technical blueprint for every Australian sprinter who followed in his wake."

In his later years, Henricks transitioned into a successful business career in the United States while maintaining close ties to his homeland's swimming community. His passing marks the end of an era for the Melbourne 1956 veterans, leaving behind a legacy of excellence that continues to inspire the nation’s current crop of Olympic contenders.
