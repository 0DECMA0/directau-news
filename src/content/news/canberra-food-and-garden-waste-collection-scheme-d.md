The ACT Government has officially deferred the territory-wide rollout of its food and garden waste collection scheme for a third time, extending the final deadline to 2030. Originally slated for a 2023 commencement, the Food Organics and Garden Organics (FOGO) initiative is designed to divert household organic waste from landfill and convert it into high-grade compost. This latest postponement represents a significant setback for the capital's waste management strategy and its broader environmental milestones.

Government officials cited the necessity for robust industrial infrastructure as the primary driver behind the delay, specifically the requirement for a large-scale composting facility capable of handling the city's total organic output. While a pilot program continues to operate in the Belconnen district, the remainder of Canberra remains reliant on existing waste streams. The revised 2030 target now aligns with federal waste objectives, yet it leaves a substantial seven-year gap in the local transition toward a circular economy.

> "This persistent shifting of goalposts suggests a systemic disconnect between ambitious environmental policy and the practical realities of industrial-scale infrastructure delivery."

Critics and environmental advocates argue that the repeated delays undermine public confidence in the Territory's ability to meet its net-zero emissions targets. By the time the scheme is fully operational, the ACT will have spent nearly a decade in a transitional phase, during which thousands of tonnes of organic matter will continue to contribute to methane emissions in landfill. The government maintains that rushing the rollout without adequate processing capacity would risk the long-term viability of the entire recycling ecosystem.
