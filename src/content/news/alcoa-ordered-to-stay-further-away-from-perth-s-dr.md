The Western Australian Government has issued a formal directive to US mining titan Alcoa, mandating the relocation of its bauxite operations further from Perth’s primary drinking water catchments. This regulatory intervention follows months of intensifying public and political scrutiny regarding the potential long-term risks posed to the metropolitan area's essential water infrastructure by heavy industrial activity in the Darling Range.

For decades, Alcoa has maintained a significant footprint within these sensitive zones, contributing substantially to the state's economy. However, recent environmental assessments and community concerns regarding sediment run-off and catchment integrity have prompted a shift in the balance between resource extraction and public health safeguards. The new exclusion zones are designed to provide a more robust buffer for the Serpentine and North Dandalup dams.

> "The tension between sovereign resource extraction and the preservation of our most fundamental natural asset has reached a critical tipping point in Western Australia."

While Alcoa has publically reaffirmed its commitment to world-class rehabilitation standards and strict environmental compliance, the state’s decision signals a hardening stance on water security. Industry analysts suggest this move could set a precedent for other extractive operations situated near vital infrastructure, as the government prioritises the resilience of the water supply in a drying climate.
