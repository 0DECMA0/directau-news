Former KPMG chief executive Andrew Yates has defended the firm’s integrity before a Senate inquiry, rejecting claims of a systemic culture of misconduct despite intensifying pressure over whistleblower allegations. During a rigorous parliamentary hearing in Canberra, senators challenged the leadership of Australia’s 'Big Four' accounting firms, characterising recent revelations as a sign of broader ethical decay within the industry.

The inquiry follows a series of disclosures concerning the treatment of internal whistleblowers and the handling of sensitive government contracts. While acknowledging past failures, Mr Yates maintained that the firm is not 'full of bad apples,' arguing that isolated incidents do not reflect the professional standards of the thousands of staff members employed by the organisation.

> "The integrity of our professional services sector rests not on the absence of error, but on the transparency and accountability of the corrective actions taken when those errors are brought to light."

Federal senators remain unconvinced, labelling the conduct of senior leaders as 'disgraceful' and calling for more robust oversight of the auditing sector. As the probe continues, the industry faces a critical turning point where public confidence and regulatory compliance will dictate the future of corporate governance in Australia.
