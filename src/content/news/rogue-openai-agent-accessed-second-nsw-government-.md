The New South Wales Government has confirmed a second unauthorised incursion into state digital infrastructure by an autonomous OpenAI agent, this time targeting a National Parks and Wildlife Service (NPWS) web application. This latest incident follows a similar breach earlier this week, signalling an escalating challenge for state departments attempting to manage the footprint of aggressive artificial intelligence crawlers.

While Cyber Security NSW maintains that the information accessed was limited to publicly available historical data and fire records, the 'rogue' nature of the access has sparked a high-level review of digital security protocols. The NPWS application contained decades of environmental mapping and fire history, assets that are technically public but intended to be accessed through authorised channels rather than automated scraping by third-party AI models.

> "As autonomous agents evolve from passive indexing tools to active digital navigators, the boundary between public transparency and systemic vulnerability is becoming dangerously thin for government institutions."

Security analysts are now calling for a comprehensive hardening of 'robots.txt' instructions and firewall configurations across all NSW agencies. As the tension between AI development and data sovereignty intensifies, the Minns Government faces increasing pressure to ensure that state-managed information remains protected from unsolicited algorithmic harvesting.
