The former Tweed Valley Hospital site is poised for a significant transformation, with plans unveiled to deliver approximately 800 new dwellings in a move designed to alleviate the region’s chronic housing shortage. This strategic shift towards higher density living marks a pivotal moment for the Tweed, as the local government attempts to balance urban growth with the urgent needs of a growing population.

Initial reactions to the proposal have been largely positive, with urban planners and council members acknowledging that traditional sprawl is no longer a viable solution. However, the enthusiasm is tempered by a stern warning from community advocates who argue that the project must look beyond mere market-rate supply to address the underlying social pressures facing local residents.

> "While increasing supply is a necessary step, the true measure of success for this redevelopment will be whether it offers a genuine lifeline to those currently priced out of the Tweed community."

As the planning process moves into its next phase, the spotlight remains firmly on the developer's commitment to inclusive growth. With the regional rental market under unprecedented strain, the pressure is on to ensure that this landmark redevelopment serves as a blueprint for sustainable, equitable housing rather than just a commercial victory for the private sector.
