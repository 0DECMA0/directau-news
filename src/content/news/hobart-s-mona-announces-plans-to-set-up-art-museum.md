David Walsh, the eccentric visionary behind Hobart’s transformative Museum of Old and New Art (Mona), has officially formalised plans to export his provocative brand of curation to Southeast Asia. An agreement has been signed to establish Mona Bangkok in Thailand, marking a significant international expansion for the Tasmanian cultural powerhouse that has long anchored the island state's tourism economy.

The new venture is slated to mirror the subversive and anti-establishment spirit of its Australian predecessor, which redefined the global museum landscape with its unconventional displays and subterranean architecture. While specific site details remain under wraps, the Bangkok iteration is promised to be as unique as the Berriedale original, signaling a bold cross-cultural experiment in the heart of the Thai capital’s evolving arts scene.

> "The export of Mona’s radical ethos suggests that the 'Walsh effect' is no longer merely a local economic miracle, but a global commodity in the business of intellectual disruption."

Industry analysts view the move as a strategic pivot towards the burgeoning Asian art market, leveraging Mona's cult-like prestige to tap into Thailand’s creative sectors. As the Hobart site continues to stir public discourse, this international expansion stands as a testament to the enduring influence of Australian private patronage and its capacity to challenge traditional institutional norms on the world stage.
