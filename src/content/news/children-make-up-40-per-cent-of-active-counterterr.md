The Australian Federal Police (AFP) has issued a stark warning regarding the rapid radicalisation of minors, revealing that children now account for 40 per cent of all active counterterrorism investigations across the nation. This unprecedented surge in youth involvement marks a significant shift in the domestic security landscape, as federal authorities grapple with an increasingly young demographic being drawn into high-risk ideologies.

Intelligence data highlights a harrowing digital reality where vulnerable youngsters are exposed to violent extremist material in as little as 23 minutes after going online. This compressed window for potential grooming and radicalisation has prompted the AFP to sound the alarm on the speed at which social media algorithms and unmonitored platforms can funnel harmful content to Australian adolescents.

> "The digital frontier has effectively bypassed traditional security perimeters, delivering high-risk ideologies directly into the palms of our youth with alarming surgical precision."

In response to these escalating figures, law enforcement agencies are calling for heightened parental vigilance and a coordinated community approach to digital literacy. As the average age of those facing prosecution continues to fall, the focus remains on disrupting the online supply chains of illicit material and strengthening the resilience of young Australians against targeted manipulation.
