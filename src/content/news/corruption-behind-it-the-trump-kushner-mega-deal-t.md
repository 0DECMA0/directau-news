The planned transformation of Albania’s pristine coastline into a luxury enclave by Jared Kushner and Ivanka Trump has ignited a firestorm of local opposition and allegations of institutional impropriety. As the Balkan nation experiences a surge in budget tourism, the proposed high-end redevelopment of Sazan Island—a former military stronghold—is being viewed by residents not as an economic boon, but as a contentious seizure of national heritage by foreign interests.

Affinity Partners, the private equity firm headed by Mr. Kushner, is seeking to capitalise on the region’s growing status as a Mediterranean jewel, yet the deal has been shadowed by claims of a lack of transparency from grassroots organisations. Critics argue the fast-tracked nature of the negotiations bypasses standard environmental protections and community consultation, raising significant questions about the influence of political dynasties in international real estate dealings.

> "When private profit meets political pedigree in the world’s last untouched frontiers, the line between genuine foreign investment and the erosion of national sovereignty becomes dangerously thin."

While the Albanian government maintains that such high-calibre investments are essential for modernising the domestic economy, the sentiment on the ground remains fiercely resistant. For the locals, the arrival of the Trump-Kushner brand represents a modern 'discovery' that ignores the rights of existing inhabitants, potentially setting a concerning precedent for how global elites interact with emerging markets under the guise of progress.
