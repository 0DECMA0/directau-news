The Federal Government is set to overhaul the Small-scale Renewable Energy Scheme (SRES) in a definitive move to slash upfront solar costs for Australian businesses. This expansion aims to bridge the significant gap between high residential uptake and lagging commercial adoption, marking a pivotal shift in the nation’s energy transition strategy.

Current incentives have largely favoured the residential sector, leaving many small and medium-sized enterprises (SMEs) to navigate volatile energy markets without the benefit of large-scale solar offsets. By adjusting the capacity thresholds, the government intends to provide immediate financial relief to businesses facing mounting overheads, effectively incentivising a private-sector push toward carbon neutrality.

> "True energy security for the Australian economy will only be achieved when our commercial industrial heartlands are as sun-powered as our suburban rooftops."

Industry peak bodies anticipate that the move will unlock significant private investment and alleviate pressure on the national grid during peak daylight hours. As the details of the expansion are finalised, the focus remains on ensuring the policy provides long-term stability for both the renewable energy industry and the broader business community.
