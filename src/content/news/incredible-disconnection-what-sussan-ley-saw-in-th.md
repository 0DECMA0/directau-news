Former Coalition leader Sussan Ley has returned to the Australian political landscape with a sobering assessment of international social dynamics following a high-level observation mission across the United States. Following her recent transition from the party's top leadership role, Ley sought to evaluate the health of democratic institutions abroad, only to find a society grappling with profound internal fractures.

The observations, gathered during a period of professional reflection, highlighted a level of civic fragmentation that Ley described as an 'incredible disconnection' from the shared national interest. By examining the stresses currently impacting American communities, the veteran MP has pivoted her focus toward the resilience of the Australian social fabric, noting that the domestic approach to integration remains significantly more robust than its overseas counterparts.

> "The erosion of common ground in major Western democracies serves as a cautionary tale for the Commonwealth; we must value our social cohesion as a strategic asset rather than a foregone conclusion."

Ley’s findings conclude that the Australian model of multiculturalism, often scrutinised at home, serves as a vital bulwark against the polarising forces she witnessed abroad. Her return signals a renewed emphasis on preserving the egalitarian values that distinguish the Australian experience in an increasingly volatile global era.
