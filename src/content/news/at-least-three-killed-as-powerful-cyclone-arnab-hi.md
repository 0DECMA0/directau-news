At least three people have lost their lives after the powerful Cyclone Arnab made landfall on India’s east coast early Wednesday morning. The weather system struck Andhra Pradesh state with significant force, flattening dozens of homes and leaving several coastal districts without power as emergency services struggle to reach affected populations.

Local authorities report that the destruction of seasonal crops has dealt a severe blow to the region's agricultural sector, further complicating the humanitarian situation. Rescue crews are currently prioritising the restoration of the power grid and providing shelter to those displaced by the surge, as the full scale of the damage continues to emerge from isolated areas.

> "The recurring ferocity of these seasonal systems highlights an urgent need for more robust coastal infrastructure to mitigate the inevitable human and economic toll of the changing climate."

While the cyclone is projected to lose intensity as it tracks further inland, the immediate focus remains on life-saving operations and the delivery of essential supplies. The international community is closely monitoring the situation as India’s disaster management agencies coordinate the recovery effort in the wake of this latest environmental crisis.
