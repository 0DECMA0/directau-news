The Australian residential property market has recorded its fifth consecutive monthly decline, as a broad-based downturn now impacts nearly every corner of the nation's capital cities. Data released by property analytics firm Cotality reveals that 93 per cent of metropolitan suburbs are experiencing a softening in valuations, driven by historically low buyer engagement and tightening credit conditions across the board.

The scale of the current correction is becoming increasingly evident, with the weakness no longer confined to the premium markets of Sydney and Melbourne. As interest rate pressures continue to weigh heavily on household budgets, potential purchasers are remaining on the sidelines, leading to a significant build-up in listing durations and a distinct shift in leverage toward the few buyers still active in the current climate.

> "The current correction reflects a fundamental recalibration of the Australian housing market, as the era of easy credit gives way to a challenging new reality for homeowners in ninety-three per cent of our urban suburbs."

While some regional pockets maintain a level of resilience, the overarching trend suggests a period of sustained consolidation for the national property landscape. Industry economists suggest that until buyer sentiment recovers and the interest rate trajectory stabilises, the downward pressure on valuations across capital city markets is likely to persist well into the coming financial year.
