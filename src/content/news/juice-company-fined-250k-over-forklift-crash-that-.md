The Real Juice Company has been ordered to pay a $250,000 fine by the Industrial Relations Commission following a severe workplace accident that resulted in a female employee suffering permanent, life-altering injuries. The court heard that the worker lost several toes after being struck by a forklift at the company’s processing facility. This substantial penalty follows a rigorous investigation into the firm's safety culture and operational standards.

Evidence presented during the hearing revealed a troubling history of safety breaches, noting that this was the third forklift-related incident at the site. Furthermore, it was established that the operators involved had received no formal training or safety inductions prior to the event. This systemic failure to adhere to mandatory workplace health and safety protocols highlights a significant lapse in corporate responsibility and duty of care toward the workforce.

> "When institutional negligence intersects with high-risk industrial machinery, the inevitable result is a human cost that no monetary figure can truly compensate."

In delivering the sentence, the Commission emphasised that the lack of oversight was particularly egregious given the prior incidents. The ruling serves as a stern warning to Australian industrial operators regarding the non-negotiable nature of staff training and the heavy legal consequences of ignoring workplace hazards. The company now faces intensified scrutiny as it is mandated to overhaul its safety management systems to prevent further harm.
