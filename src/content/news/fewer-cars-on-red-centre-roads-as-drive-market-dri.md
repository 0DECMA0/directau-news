The heart of Australia’s tourism industry is facing a severe downturn as escalating fuel prices deter travellers from embarking on long-distance road trips through the Northern Territory’s Red Centre.

Recent data from operators along the Stuart Highway indicate a sharp contraction in the drive market, with some wayside inns situated around Alice Springs reporting a staggering 75 per cent decline in overnight bookings compared to previous seasons. This trend signals a significant shift in consumer behaviour as the financial burden of transit outweighs the appeal of the traditional outback trek.

> "The thinning traffic on our regional arteries is a grim barometer for the broader economic pressures currently reshaping the Australian dream of the great open road."

Industry stakeholders are now calling for a closer examination of regional support mechanisms as the local economy, which relies heavily on the influx of interstate visitors, struggles to maintain viability amidst these unprecedented operational headwinds.
