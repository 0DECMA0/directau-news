A groundbreaking study has confirmed that 31 former professional rugby league players have been diagnosed with chronic traumatic encephalopathy (CTE), a progressive and debilitating brain disease. The findings, sourced from the Australian Sports Brain Bank, have sent shockwaves through the sporting landscape, providing a sobering look at the long-term neurological risks associated with Australia's most popular contact code.

The research indicates a clear correlation between years of high-impact collisions and the onset of permanent brain damage. As the medical community continues to map the impact of repetitive head trauma, this data forces a difficult conversation for administrators and fans alike regarding the duty of care owed to those who have played at the elite level for decades.

> "The collision between our national sporting identity and the harsh reality of neurological health has reached a tipping point that the game can no longer afford to ignore."

In response to the report, calls for more rigorous concussion protocols and enhanced post-career support have intensified. While the National Rugby League has introduced significant rule changes in recent seasons to mitigate risk, this latest evidence suggests that the sport must remain vigilant in evolving its safety standards to protect the cognitive future of its athletes.
