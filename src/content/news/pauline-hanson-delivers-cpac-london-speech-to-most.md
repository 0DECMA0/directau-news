Senator Pauline Hanson’s international tour has concluded with a significant lack of fanfare, as the One Nation leader addressed a largely vacant auditorium at the Conservative Political Action Conference (CPAC) in London on Saturday. Attendance figures indicate that only approximately 120 individuals were present for the keynote, marking a remarkably subdued end to her diplomatic mission through Britain and Europe.

The event, which serves as a global gathering point for right-wing political figures, failed to draw the substantial crowds typically associated with high-profile Australian political exports. Despite her long-standing presence in the Australian Senate and her history of polarising rhetoric, the London engagement suggests a limited appetite for her particular brand of nationalism within the current European political landscape.

> "The silence of a near-empty room often speaks louder than the most provocative stump speech, highlighting the widening chasm between domestic notoriety and international relevance."

This muted reception comes at a pivotal time for One Nation as the party seeks to maintain its domestic influence ahead of the next federal election cycle. As Senator Hanson prepares to return to Australia, the low turnout in London raises significant questions about the efficacy of her international outreach and the degree to which her platform resonates beyond her established supporter base at home.
