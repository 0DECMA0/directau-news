Resources Minister Madeleine King has forcefully intervened in the escalating debate over Western Australia's GST share, labelling a recent interim report that suggested a funding cut as "clearly incorrect." The Minister’s remarks serve as a definitive rebuff to recommendations that sought to wind back the 75-cent floor, a mechanism designed to protect the state’s revenue despite its substantial mining royalties.

Addressing the growing friction between state capitals, Minister King confirmed that the resource-rich jurisdiction maintains the full confidence and backing of the Prime Minister. The controversy follows months of quiet lobbying from eastern states, who have argued that the 2018 deal unfairly advantages the West at a time of record commodity prices and national fiscal tightening.

> "The GST floor is no longer merely a fiscal adjustment; it has become the linchpin of a fragile economic federation where the surplus of the West balances the ledger of the Commonwealth."

As the Albanese Government moves to provide certainty to the mining sector and Western Australian voters, the intervention signals a firm stance against any immediate structural changes to the distribution model. For now, the federal leadership appears committed to maintaining the status quo, prioritising national stability over the volatile demands of inter-state financial competition.
