Home Affairs Minister Tony Burke has intervened in the ongoing Bondi royal commission, confirming that a former ASIO operative will be permitted to provide testimony in an open forum. This decision marks the Commonwealth’s first formal public position on the matter, addressing the delicate balance between safeguarding national intelligence and ensuring total transparency for the families affected by the tragedy earlier this year.

The Minister's remarks follow weeks of legal deliberation regarding the classified nature of the agent's previous work and its relevance to the security lapses at Bondi Junction. By allowing the evidence to be heard publicly, the government aims to dispel concerns that the inquiry’s reach would be limited by the 'closed-door' protocols typically associated with the intelligence community.

> "In the pursuit of justice, the shield of national security must be wielded with precision, ensuring it protects the state without obscuring the truth that the public rightfully demands."

While specific operational methods remain protected, the core testimony is expected to provide critical insights into the threat assessments conducted prior to the incident. The royal commission is slated to continue its proceedings in the coming days, with this testimony anticipated to be a pivotal moment in the quest for accountability and future prevention.
