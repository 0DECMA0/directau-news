Spain has entered a three-day period of national mourning as the confirmed death toll from a catastrophic high-speed rail collision reaches 74. The incident, marking the nation's second significant railway tragedy this year, has prompted a state of profound shock and immediate calls for a rigorous investigation into safety standards.

Emergency response teams worked through the night at the wreckage site, while authorities confirmed the devastating scale of the casualties. Flags are flying at half-mast across government buildings as the administration pledges full support to those affected by the loss of life and the survivors undergoing treatment.

> "This catastrophe represents more than an infrastructural failure; it is a profound test of a nation's resilience and its commitment to the safety of its citizens."

Condolences from international leaders have begun to arrive as the country attempts to process the magnitude of the event. As the investigation into the high-speed impact continues, the priority remains the identification of the remaining victims and the long-term support for a community devastated by one of Europe's most severe transit disasters.
