The cancellation of a high-profile promotional event by a South Korean cosmetic surgery firm in Sydney has reignited concerns regarding the safety of 'medical tourism' and the regulation of foreign practitioners on Australian soil. The Australian Society of Plastic Surgeons (ASPS) issued a stern warning after discovering that doctors scheduled to consult with prospective patients were not registered to practise within the Commonwealth.

Local experts highlighted the significant risks associated with undergoing invasive procedures overseas, where Australian clinical standards and rigorous follow-up care protocols do not apply. The event, which aimed to facilitate face lifts and other aesthetic enhancements, was abruptly withdrawn following intense scrutiny from medical watchdogs and health authorities regarding the legal standing of the visiting clinicians.

> "The allure of a more affordable procedure often blinds the public to the high cost of non-standardised post-operative care and the absence of local medical accountability."

The incident serves as a critical reminder of the legal and health complexities involved in offshore medical contracts. While the appeal of reduced costs remains a significant driver for the industry, the ASPS continues to urge Australians to prioritise practitioners subject to local disciplinary boards and strict safety frameworks to ensure long-term wellbeing.
