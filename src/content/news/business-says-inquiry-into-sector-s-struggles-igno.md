The Productivity Commission’s interim report on business dynamism has drawn sharp rebukes from industry leaders following government directives that strictly prohibited the consideration of taxation or industrial relations frameworks. The findings, intended to map a path toward a more robust Australian economy, are being viewed by the business community as fundamentally incomplete due to these foundational omissions.

Tasked with investigating the stagnation within the domestic private sector, the Commission was hamstrung by a narrow remit that excluded the two most significant levers of fiscal policy. Major peak bodies have expressed frustration, suggesting that any analysis of business viability that fails to account for the current tax burden or the complexities of the workplace relations system ignores the practical realities facing Australian employers today.

> "To examine the mechanics of national prosperity while cordoning off the very fiscal foundations upon which firms are built is to settle for a partial diagnosis of a systemic malaise."

While the interim report highlights potential gains through digital integration and minor regulatory streamlining, the consensus among sector advocates remains that genuine dynamism requires a more holistic approach. As the inquiry moves toward its final submission, the disconnect between the government’s restricted scope and the industry’s demand for structural reform remains the defining narrative of the debate.
