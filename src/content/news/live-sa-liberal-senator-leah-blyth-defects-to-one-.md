In a seismic shift for South Australian politics, Senator Leah Blyth has officially resigned from the Liberal Party to join the ranks of Pauline Hanson’s One Nation. The move, confirmed this morning, follows a period of intense internal friction, with Blyth declaring she has lost confidence in the Coalition’s ability to lead the country effectively in the current climate.

The defection is understood to be the culmination of a fractured relationship between the Senator and SA Liberal President Alex Antic. Sources close to the matter suggest that ideological disparities and administrative disputes reached a breaking point, leaving Blyth to conclude that her political future lay outside the traditional party structure she has represented in the federal chamber.

> "A party's integrity is often measured by its ability to hold its centre; when that core erodes, the migration toward the periphery becomes an inevitable political reality for those seeking a clearer ideological mandate."

Blyth’s departure presents a fresh challenge for the Liberal leadership as they struggle to maintain a unified front ahead of the next federal election. As she settles onto the crossbench under the One Nation banner, the move is expected to significantly bolster the minor party's influence over key legislative votes, forcing a strategic recalibration within the Senate.
