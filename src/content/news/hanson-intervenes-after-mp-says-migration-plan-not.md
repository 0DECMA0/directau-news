One Nation leader Pauline Hanson has swiftly intervened to re-establish her party's hardline stance on immigration following contradictory remarks made by MP David Farley. During an appearance on the ABC’s Insiders program, Mr. Farley suggested that One Nation’s proposed migration cuts were "not too different" to those currently being pursued by the Albanese Labor Government, a comparison that threatened to undermine the party’s primary policy differentiator.

Senator Hanson moved quickly to correct the record, asserting that One Nation remains committed to a significantly more drastic reduction in net overseas migration than the major parties. While Labor has flagged intentions to return migration levels to pre-pandemic norms, Hanson clarified that her party’s framework aims to slash current intake rates by approximately half, maintaining a clear ideological distance from the status quo.

> "The integrity of a minor party’s brand relies entirely on its ability to offer a genuine alternative, making any perceived alignment with the political establishment a significant strategic risk."

This internal friction highlights the mounting pressure on One Nation to safeguard its core platform as both Labor and the Coalition shift toward more restrictive border policies. As the federal election cycle intensifies, the party must now work to ensure its messaging remains cohesive and distinct in a crowded political landscape focused heavily on housing and cost-of-living pressures.
