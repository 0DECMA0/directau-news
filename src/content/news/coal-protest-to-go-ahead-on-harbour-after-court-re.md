The NSW Supreme Court has granted legal clearance for a major climate protest to proceed on Newcastle Harbour this weekend, following a failed bid by the New South Wales Police to have the event prohibited. Justice Robertson dismissed the police application on Friday, effectively sanctioning the 'Rising Tide' maritime demonstration which aims to disrupt operations at the world’s largest coal export terminal.

Police had raised significant safety concerns regarding the scale of the flotilla and its impact on international shipping channels, arguing that the gathering posed an unacceptable risk to public order. However, the court's decision reaffirms the fundamental right to peaceful assembly in New South Wales, even when such actions target critical economic infrastructure. Organisers are now preparing for thousands of participants to take to the water in kayaks and small vessels, creating a significant logistical challenge for local authorities.

> "This ruling highlights the delicate equilibrium between the democratic right to public dissent and the state's mandate to maintain maritime security and industrial flow."

In response to the judgment, NSW Police confirmed they would maintain a significant presence at the harbour to monitor the event and ensure the safety of all involved. While the protest is now legally authorised, authorities have warned that any activities deviating from the approved plan or compromising the safety of the shipping lanes could still result in law enforcement intervention. The event is expected to be one of the largest environmental demonstrations in the Hunter region's history.
