Telstra executives are set to front a rigorous Senate inquiry following a catastrophic nationwide network failure earlier this week. The outage, which paralysed essential transport networks and halted millions of business transactions, has prompted federal lawmakers to demand immediate accountability regarding the resilience of the nation's critical telecommunications infrastructure.

The disruption left thousands of commuters stranded as ticketing systems failed and forced retail outlets to revert to cash-only operations during peak trading hours. Both government and opposition leaders have expressed bipartisan concern over the fragility of the network, particularly as the country moves increasingly toward a digital-first economy.

> "This failure represents more than a technical glitch; it highlights a systemic vulnerability in the digital backbone that sustains the Australian economy."

The upcoming inquiry will focus on the telco's redundancy protocols and the specific failure of backup systems to mitigate the impact. While services have since been restored, the long-term reputational damage and the potential for increased regulatory oversight remain a primary concern for both shareholders and the Australian public.
