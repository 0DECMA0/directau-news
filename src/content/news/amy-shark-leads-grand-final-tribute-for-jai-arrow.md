Tens of thousands of spectators at Accor Stadium fell into a poignant silence before erupting in a unified 'arrow' gesture during the NRL Grand Final, as ARIA-winner Amy Shark led a moving tribute to Jai Arrow and Nathan Charles. The display of solidarity served as a powerful prelude to the season’s decider, honouring two icons of Australian sport currently facing recent diagnoses of motor neuron disease (MND).

South Sydney Rabbitohs star Jai Arrow and former Wallabies hooker Nathan Charles completed a lap of honour, visibly moved by the outpouring of support from the sporting community. The diagnosis of both athletes earlier this year has sent shockwaves through the codes, prompting a massive fundraising and awareness effort that culminated in tonight's public display of collective strength and affection.

> "In the gladiatorial arena of elite sport, there are rare moments where the scoreboard becomes irrelevant, replaced by a profound collective reminder of our shared resilience against an unforgiving condition."

The initiative, which saw fans from both sides of the fence uniting under the arrow symbol, underscores a growing movement within Australian sport to support those battling the debilitating neurodegenerative condition. As the match commenced, the message was clear: while the rivalry on the pitch remains fierce, the brotherhood of the sporting world remains unbreakable in the face of such adversity.
