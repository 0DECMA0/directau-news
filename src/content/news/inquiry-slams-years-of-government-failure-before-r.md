A scathing parliamentary inquiry has delivered a stinging rebuke to the Tasmanian government over its long-term management of the sand flathead fishery, citing a decade of systemic failure. The lower house committee's report concludes that the recent fishing ban across the state’s south was a panicked response to a crisis that had been entirely avoidable, had authorities acted on clear signs of declining stocks years earlier.

Evidence presented to the inquiry suggests that the Department of Natural Resources and Environment Tasmania failed to conduct adequate stock assessments or engage with recreational fishers until the situation reached a critical point. The resulting ban, implemented with minimal notice, has been described as a failure of both policy and public consultation, leaving local communities sidelined and frustrated by the sudden loss of access to a staple species.

> "This report exposes a fundamental breakdown in environmental stewardship, where years of administrative neglect were eventually met with a blunt regulatory instrument that bypassed the democratic process of consultation."

In its recommendations, the committee has called for an immediate overhaul of marine resource management to prevent such administrative lapses from recurring. The government now faces the arduous task of rebuilding trust with the public while ensuring that future sustainability measures are rooted in robust data and genuine community engagement, rather than last-minute reactive measures.
