The Supreme Court of New South Wales has been presented with a harrowing Triple Zero recording as the trial of former police constable Beau Lamarre-Condon enters a critical phase. The audio, captured from the mobile phone of Luke Davies, provides a stark chronological marker for the moments the Crown alleges he and partner Jesse Baird were fatally attacked in their Paddington home.

Legal proceedings turned somber as the jury listened to the brief call, which the prosecution contends is a vital piece of evidence linking the defendant to the scene at the time of the incident. The former officer stands accused of using his departmental firearm in the tragedy, a case that has sparked significant national conversation regarding police accountability and domestic security.

> "The weight of a Triple Zero call in a courtroom serves as a haunting reminder that behind every legal exhibit lies a human tragedy that demands the highest standard of judicial scrutiny."

While the defence has previously raised arguments regarding the defendant's state of mind, the court remains focused on the forensic and digital timeline established by investigators. As the trial continues, further testimonies from forensic experts are expected to shed light on the actions taken by the accused following the events of that February morning.
