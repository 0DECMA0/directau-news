The Albanese Government is exploring a potential prohibition of smart glasses within Commonwealth workplaces as part of a sweeping new regulatory framework designed to govern Australia’s rapidly evolving artificial intelligence sector. Minister for Industry and Science Ed Husic has launched a public consultation process to address the mounting security concerns posed by wearable technologies capable of covert recording and data transmission in high-security environments.

Under the proposed guidelines, the use of augmented reality (AR) and smart eyewear could be restricted or entirely banned for public servants to mitigate risks associated with unauthorised data harvesting. This move signals a hardening stance from Labor as it seeks to balance the benefits of technological innovation against the imperative of safeguarding institutional integrity and individual privacy rights across the federal bureaucracy.

> "The intersection of ubiquitous wearable surveillance and national security demands a rigorous recalibration of our traditional digital privacy boundaries."

Industry stakeholders have expressed concern that overly stringent regulations may stifle the adoption of emerging tech, yet Canberra remains focused on establishing 'high-risk' classifications for AI-enabled devices. As the consultation period progresses, the government is expected to refine these rules to ensure that the Commonwealth’s information architecture remains resilient against the sophisticated challenges of the modern digital age.
