Nepal has formally issued a massive $7 billion appeal for reconstruction following the catastrophic flooding that decimated the nation in late August. Government officials in Kathmandu are framing the recovery effort as a matter of global climate justice, arguing that wealthier nations must accept a "shared responsibility" for disasters intensified by global warming.

The August 26 weather events caused unprecedented damage to critical infrastructure, housing, and agriculture, leaving the Himalayan state with a recovery bill that far exceeds its domestic capacity. As the nation grapples with the scale of the loss, the push for international financial intervention highlights the growing tension between developing nations and the industrialised world over the true cost of climate-fuelled volatility.

> "The staggering price tag of this recovery serves as a stark warning: the bill for climate inaction is no longer a future projection, but a present-day debt being settled by those least responsible for the crisis."

In Canberra and other global capitals, the request is being viewed as a litmus test for the 'loss and damage' mechanisms established at recent climate summits. Nepal's authorities have indicated that without significant external capital, the path to rebuilding resilient communities will remain out of reach, potentially leading to long-term economic instability in the region.
