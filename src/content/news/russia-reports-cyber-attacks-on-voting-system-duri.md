Russian authorities have confirmed a series of coordinated cyber-attacks targeting the nation's digital voting infrastructure and communication networks as citizens participate in the three-day parliamentary election. Official reports indicate that the distributed denial-of-service (DDoS) attempts were aimed at disrupting the electoral process across several regions, though officials maintain that the core integrity of the ballot remains intact.

The disruptions come at a critical juncture for the Kremlin, as the multi-day polling format was designed to facilitate participation amidst ongoing logistical challenges. These technical interferences have reportedly impacted the Central Election Commission's servers and several key telecommunications providers, leading to intermittent outages in data transmission from remote polling stations.

> "The digitisation of democracy has turned the humble ballot box into a frontline for invisible conflict, where the primary casualty is often public confidence in the system itself."

While the source of the interference has not been formally identified, Moscow has signalled that security protocols have been escalated to neutralise further threats. As the count progresses, international observers and cyber-security experts are closely monitoring the resilience of the Russian network against these sophisticated digital incursions.
