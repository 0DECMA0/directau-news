Leading advocacy groups across Victoria are demanding that major political parties significantly bolster their election commitments to address the escalating domestic and family violence crisis. As the state election approaches, frontline service providers argue that current policy proposals are insufficient to meet the unprecedented demand for emergency support and long-term prevention initiatives.

The call for action follows a series of reports detailing the immense pressure on Victoria’s social infrastructure, with police responding to record numbers of domestic incidents. Stakeholders are now seeking a bipartisan, multi-year funding guarantee that ensures the safety of vulnerable citizens is prioritised over short-term political gains, moving beyond incremental budget adjustments toward systemic reform.

> "The integrity of our social contract is tested by our ability to safeguard the home; we require a generational shift in policy that treats domestic safety as a fundamental pillar of state security."

With the campaign entering its final stages, the focus remains on whether party leaders will commit to the comprehensive investment required to reduce the cycle of harm. Advocates insist that without a definitive and well-resourced strategy, the gap between the scale of the crisis and the available support services will continue to widen, leaving many Victorians at risk.
