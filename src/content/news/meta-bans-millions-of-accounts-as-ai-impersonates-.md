Meta has initiated a massive purge, removing millions of accounts globally as generative artificial intelligence fuels a new wave of sophisticated identity impersonation. The crackdown follows alarming reports from content creators, including an Australian woman named Alicia, who discovered a fraudulent account had harvested over 500,000 followers by publishing hyper-realistic, AI-generated images of her likeness without her consent.

The incident has sparked fresh debate regarding the efficacy of platform moderation and the legal protections afforded to digital identities in the age of automation. While the tech giant claims its automated systems are identifying and removing bad actors at an unprecedented rate, the ability of these bots to bypass initial filters and build massive audiences highlights a growing disparity between malicious AI deployment and existing defensive measures.

> "The weaponisation of likeness through synthetic media represents a fundamental shift in digital harm, where the theft of a face has become as lucrative as the theft of a fortune."

As the Australian government continues to review the Privacy Act, experts warn that the industry is playing a perpetual game of catch-up against algorithmic forgery. For local creators, the threat of being 'replaced' by a digital version of themselves is no longer a futuristic concern, but a pressing reality that demands more robust verification and swifter intervention from social media conglomerates.
