In the high-stakes theatre of Victorian politics, the traditional public debate is undergoing a quiet but deliberate disappearance. As the state nears its next election, political strategists within the major parties are increasingly prioritising risk management over the unpredictable nature of live televised scrutiny, a move that fundamentally alters the landscape of democratic engagement.

The reluctance to engage in multiple public forums signals a significant shift in how power is brokered in the modern era. By bypassing traditional debates, leaders and their advisers are effectively shielding campaigns from the potential gaffes that can pivot an entire election in a single news cycle, opting instead for curated social media outputs and controlled press conferences.

> "When the cost of a single unscripted moment outweighs the perceived value of public discourse, the silence from the podium becomes a calculated political asset."

Ultimately, this trend raises serious questions about the transparency of the parliamentary process in Victoria. While parties may view the avoidance of debates as a tactical win to protect their lead, the Victorian electorate is left with fewer opportunities to witness their prospective leaders being held to account on the critical issues defining the state's future.
