Cathie Wood, the high-profile founder of ARK Investment Management, has warned that the global economy could be bracing for interest rates exceeding 7 per cent as a result of an unprecedented artificial intelligence surge. This projection comes despite Wood’s insistence that the burgeoning technology will trigger a period of "good deflation," driven by massive productivity gains and rapid economic expansion.

While traditional economic theory suggests that deflation typically coincides with stagnation, Wood argues that the AI boom will decouple growth from price stability. The $42 billion fund manager suggests that while costs for goods and services may fall due to technological efficiencies, the sheer velocity of economic activity could force central banks to maintain higher terminal rates to manage the transition.

> "The paradox of the coming decade lies in the collision of plummeting production costs and the structural necessity for higher capital returns in a hyper-growth environment."

Market analysts remain divided on Wood’s outlook, noting that a 7 per cent cash rate would represent a significant shift from the low-interest environment that defined the last decade. As the world’s financial hubs monitor these developments, the focus remains on whether central banks will embrace this paradigm shift or lean toward traditional fiscal restraints.
