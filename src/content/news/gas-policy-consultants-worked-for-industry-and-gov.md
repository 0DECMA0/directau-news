The integrity of federal energy policy has been cast into doubt following revelations that a consultancy firm engaged to shape the Commonwealth’s national gas strategy was simultaneously receiving payment from the gas industry. The firm reportedly failed to disclose this significant conflict of interest to the Department of Industry, Science, and Resources, potentially compromising the impartiality of the data used to inform Australia’s future energy landscape.

At the heart of the matter is the dual role played by the advisors, who were tasked with providing objective insights for the government’s Future Gas Strategy while concurrently serving private interests within the same sector. Crossbench senators and transparency advocates have raised concerns that such 'double-dipping' erodes public confidence in parliamentary processes and suggests a systemic failure in the vetting of external contractors.

> "When the line between independent policy advice and corporate advocacy becomes indistinguishable, the foundational trust required for democratic governance is fundamentally compromised."

In response to the findings, the federal energy department has initiated a formal review of its procurement protocols and conflict-of-interest declarations. As Canberra navigates a complex transition toward renewable targets, the fallout from these undisclosed ties may force a broader reckoning regarding the influence of private consultants on public policy and the rigour of government oversight.
