A fully laden coal train accelerated to speeds exceeding 60 kilometres per hour without a driver at the controls in the Hunter Valley, sparking an urgent safety investigation by national rail regulators. The incident occurred during a routine crew change on a descending gradient, causing the massive consist to roll uncontrolled through a section of the New South Wales rail corridor.

Authorities from the Australian Transport Safety Bureau (ATSB) are currently scrutinising the braking protocols and mechanical fail-safes that were in place at the time of the event. The train, carrying several thousand tonnes of coal, travelled for a significant distance before eventually coming to a halt without any reports of derailment or contact with other rail traffic.

> "This breach of operational integrity highlights the thin margin for error when managing heavy haulage infrastructure in our critical regional corridors."

Local rail networks experienced minor disruptions as safety teams worked to clear the tracks and verify the integrity of the line. While no individuals were harmed during the event, the freight industry is awaiting the final findings of the ATSB report to determine if systemic failures contributed to this significant near-miss in the state's busiest coal artery.
