Australian social enterprise Who Gives A Crap has confirmed a significant data breach resulting from a vulnerability within a third-party software provider, impacting an undisclosed number of customers. The Melbourne-based organisation, renowned for its sustainable approach to household goods, informed subscribers that personal identifiers including names, contact details, and total purchase history were accessed by unauthorised actors during the incident.

While the breach allowed external access to basic customer profiles, the company has moved swiftly to reassure the public that sensitive financial data remains untouched. According to an official statement, encrypted passwords and full credit card details were not stored on the compromised system, mitigating the risk of direct identity theft or immediate financial fraud for the affected user base.

> "In the modern digital economy, a brand's reputation no longer rests solely on its own fortifications, but on the integrity of every link in its technical supply chain."

Who Gives A Crap has since rectified the security flaw and is currently working with forensic cybersecurity experts to monitor for further malicious activity. As the Australian government continues to weigh stricter penalties for data mismanagement, this event serves as a stark reminder for local consumers to remain vigilant against phishing attempts and to monitor their digital footprints for irregularities.
